# Problem Validation

We have not collected any answers yet. Everything in the "Assumptions" part is what we think, not what we found. We will only write findings after real students answer.

## 1. Why we do this

We want to check if students who live together really have the problems Birga is made for, which ones are the most important, and which features we can remove.

## 2. Our assumptions

| # | Assumption | Questions | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Shared expenses are tracked in a chat or by memory, and it becomes unclear who owes whom | Q4-Q7 | not tested |
| A2 | There is no fixed rotation for chores and some people think it is unfair | Q8-Q10 | not tested |
| A3 | Shared things run out unexpectedly or get bought twice | Q11-Q12 | not tested |
| A4 | Rent and utility bills are sometimes paid late or the split is unclear | Q13-Q14 | not tested |
| A5 | Students keep deadlines in different places and sometimes almost miss them | Q15-Q16 | not tested |
| A6 | Students would use such an app if their roommates used it too | Q19-Q20 | not tested |

## 3. How we will run it

- At least 10 university students who live with at least one other student (apartment, house or dorm). If some students who live alone answer, we will count them separately.
- Google Forms in Uzbek, and a Russian copy for students who prefer Russian. We can also ask the same questions in person.
- We tell people what it is for, that it is anonymous and that they can skip questions. We don't ask for names, phone numbers, addresses, student IDs or exact amounts of money.
- We ask about the last month or last semester, because real recent situations are better than general opinions. We don't explain our app before Q18 so the earlier answers are not influenced.

## 6. How we will summarize it

1. Write how many people answered and how many of them live with others.
2. For each question write the counts, for example "Q5: 4 of 11 had an unclear debt last month". With about 10 people percentages don't mean much, so we use counts.
3. For Q7 and Q9 write how many chose 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5, and the median.
4. For the open questions, group similar answers and count them. Keep 2-3 short quotes without names.
5. Rank the five parts of the app using Q18.
6. Update the "Status" column in part 2: supported, not supported or mixed, with the question and the numbers.

## 7. What we will change depending on the answers

We decided these rules before collecting answers, so the answers decide and not us.

- If fewer than 3 of 10 had unclear debts (Q5) and the median of Q7 is 2 or less, we keep expenses simple and don't focus on settle up.
- If the median of Q9 is 4 or more and Q10 is mostly "Yo'q", chores become a simple checklist in the presentation. The rotation stays in the app but is not the main feature.
- If Q11 and Q12 are mostly "Yo'q", the shopping list becomes less important.
- If most people live in dorms where bills are included (Q13), we hide or remove Bills.
- If most say "Yo'q" in Q19 because roommates won't join (Q20), we focus on the parts that work for one person (planner and personal spending).
- If one part is clearly the top in Q18, it becomes the main story of our presentation.
